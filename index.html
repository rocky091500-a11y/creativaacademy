From First Steps to First Grade Ready

Our Programs

Thoughtfully designed classrooms for every stage of early childhood, from 15 months through Kindergarten.

At Creativa, children learn best through relationships, exploration, and hands-on discovery. Rather than following a single script, our classrooms are built around real projects, open-ended art, movement, nature, and building — giving children room to ask questions, test ideas, and make their own connections. Teachers act as guides and collaborators, following each child's interests to deepen learning in ways that feel meaningful and joyful. This approach nurtures not just skills, but curious, confident thinkers who love to learn.

15 – 17 Months

Gentle first classroom experiences built on trust.

At this age, your toddler is discovering how much their body and voice can do — and we build our days around that sense of wonder.

What We Work Toward

Communication and Language

Beginning to use a handful of familiar words, pointing to show interest, and responding to simple, warm requests like "come here" or "all done."

Social-Emotional Development

Seeking comfort from trusted caregivers, showing affection, and starting to notice other children with curiosity.

Fine-Motor Skills

Grasping and releasing objects with more control, stacking two or three blocks, and turning board-book pages.

Gross-Motor Skills

Walking with growing confidence, beginning to climb low steps, and enjoying pushing and pulling toys.

Independence and Self-Help

Attempting to feed themselves with fingers or a spoon, helping to pull up simple clothing, and holding a cup with support.

Early Literacy

Enjoying being read to, pointing at pictures, and imitating sounds from favorite stories and songs.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Exploring cause and effect through simple actions — dropping, filling, and emptying containers.

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Touching, exploring with their senses, and engaging with textures like sand, water, and soft materials during guided sensory play.

Learn More

18 – 23 Months

Language, movement, and sensory exploration.

Toddlers this age are natural explorers, and our classrooms give them safe, rich places to test what they can do.

What We Work Toward

Communication and Language

Growing a small vocabulary, combining two words together ("more milk"), and following short two-step directions.

Social-Emotional Development

Showing pride in accomplishments, beginning parallel play alongside peers, and expressing emotions more clearly.

Fine-Motor Skills

Scribbling with crayons, stacking four or more blocks, and turning knobs or twisting simple objects.

Gross-Motor Skills

Running with more balance, climbing onto furniture, and kicking a ball with intention.

Independence and Self-Help

Using a spoon more independently, attempting to wash hands with help, and showing interest in simple routines like putting away toys.

Early Literacy

Requesting favorite books by name, joining in repeated phrases, and recognizing familiar pictures.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Sorting objects by an obvious feature like color or size, and solving simple shape puzzles with support.

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Experimenting with paint, dough, and natural materials, and beginning to represent ideas through marks and gestures.

Learn More

Early & Late Twos

Independence and imagination take center stage.

Twos are bursting with independence and big feelings, and as they near three we see longer attention spans and richer imaginations — we meet it all with patience, structure, and plenty of hands-on discovery.

What We Work Toward
Early 2s

Communication and Language

Speaking in short phrases, asking simple questions, and naming familiar people, animals, and objects.

Social-Emotional Development

Beginning to share with encouragement, expressing preferences, and learning simple names for feelings.

Fine-Motor Skills

Holding crayons with a developing grip, stringing large beads, and beginning to use child-safe scissors with guidance.

Gross-Motor Skills

Jumping with both feet, climbing playground equipment, and practicing riding toys and coordinated movement.

Independence and Self-Help

Feeding themselves independently, attempting to dress with help, and starting simple toileting routines when ready.

Early Literacy

Retelling parts of a favorite story, recognizing their name, and enjoying rhymes and repetition.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Counting a few objects aloud, matching simple shapes, and comparing sizes ("big" and "small").

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Building with blocks, exploring nature materials outdoors, and creating open-ended art with guidance rather than a set outcome.

Late 2s

Communication and Language

Using longer sentences, asking "why" questions, and engaging in simple back-and-forth conversation.

Social-Emotional Development

Playing cooperatively for short periods, showing empathy toward friends, and managing transitions with support.

Fine-Motor Skills

Working toward drawing recognizable shapes, using scissors with more control, and completing simple puzzles independently.

Gross-Motor Skills

Walking up and down stairs with alternating feet, throwing and catching with more accuracy, and working toward balancing on one foot.

Independence and Self-Help

Washing and drying hands mostly independently, working toward managing simple fasteners, and following a two- or three-step routine.

Early Literacy

Working toward recognizing some letters, especially in their name, and "reading" familiar books through pictures and memory.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Counting up to five with growing accuracy and working toward sorting objects by more than one feature.

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Engaging in pretend play with story lines, experimenting with mixed materials, and building simple structures with intention.

Classroom Readiness

Participating in short group activities, following classroom routines with reminders, and sitting for brief group times.

Learn More

Early & Late Threes

Project-based discovery, the Reggio way.

Threes bring big questions and bigger imaginations, and as children near four we see more collaborative projects and richer conversation woven into every day of play.

What We Work Toward
Early 3s

Communication and Language

Speaking in full sentences, describing recent events, and using language to solve small conflicts with guidance.

Social-Emotional Development

Taking turns with support, forming early friendships, and beginning to name and manage strong feelings.

Fine-Motor Skills

Drawing simple people or objects, cutting along a line, and using tools like glue sticks and tongs with control.

Gross-Motor Skills

Climbing with confidence, hopping on one foot briefly, and coordinating movement during group games.

Independence and Self-Help

Dressing with minimal help, building independence with toileting and personal-care routines, and completing familiar routines from memory.

Early Literacy

Recognizing several letters and sounds, showing interest in "writing," and retelling stories in order.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Counting and comparing small groups of objects, recognizing basic shapes and patterns, and beginning simple measurement ("more," "less").

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Leading their own pretend-play scenarios, exploring open-ended art and building materials, and investigating nature through hands-on projects.

Classroom Readiness

Following multi-step classroom routines, participating in group discussions, and transitioning between activities with growing ease.

Late 3s

Communication and Language

Using detailed sentences, asking thoughtful questions, and expressing ideas clearly to peers and teachers.

Social-Emotional Development

Cooperating on shared projects, showing consideration for others' feelings, and using words to resolve disagreements with support.

Fine-Motor Skills

Drawing with more detail and control, cutting shapes accurately, and beginning to form some letters.

Gross-Motor Skills

Running, jumping, and climbing with coordination, and participating confidently in group physical games.

Independence and Self-Help

Managing personal care tasks independently, organizing their own belongings, and following classroom expectations with fewer reminders.

Early Literacy

Working toward recognizing many letters and common sounds, attempting to write their name, and predicting what happens next in a story.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Counting to ten and beyond, recognizing and extending patterns, and solving simple problems using objects.

Creativity, Sensory Exploration, and Hands-On Learning

Planning and building multi-step creations, engaging in collaborative art and nature projects, and investigating questions through hands-on exploration.

Classroom Readiness

Sitting for longer group lessons, following multi-step directions, and taking on small classroom responsibilities.

VPK

Florida's free Voluntary Pre-K, taught with heart.

Creativa's VPK goes beyond basic readiness. We build strong early literacy, math, communication, independence, critical thinking, and classroom skills so children enter kindergarten confident and well prepared — while honoring that every child develops these skills in their own way and time.

What We Work Toward
Advanced VPK — Kindergarten-Level Preparation

Communication and Language

Speaking in detailed, organized sentences, engaging in extended conversations, and using language to explain thinking and solve problems.

Social-Emotional Development

Working through conflicts using words, showing empathy and leadership in group settings, and managing emotions with increasing independence.

Fine-Motor Skills

Writing letters and numbers with control, using tools precisely for cutting and building, and completing detailed art and construction projects.

Gross-Motor Skills

Demonstrating strong coordination in running, climbing, and group games, and participating confidently in structured physical activities.

Independence and Self-Help

Managing personal care, belongings, and daily routines with little to no reminders, and taking ownership of classroom responsibilities.

Early Literacy

Recognizing letters and sounds fluently, beginning to blend sounds into simple words, writing their name and familiar words, and retelling stories with detail and sequence.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Counting, comparing, and beginning simple addition and subtraction concepts through hands-on materials, recognizing patterns, shapes, and early measurement concepts.

Creativity, Critical Thinking, and Hands-On Learning

Leading extended projects, asking investigative questions, testing ideas, and applying creative problem-solving across art, building, and nature-based exploration.

Classroom Readiness

Following multi-step routines independently, participating actively in group instruction, listening attentively, and demonstrating the focus and self-regulation kindergarten classrooms expect.

Kindergarten

Continue the Creativa journey into big-kid learning.

Our kindergarten classroom builds on everything children have explored before, deepening academic skills while keeping curiosity, creativity, and connection at the center of learning.

What We Work Toward

Communication and Language

Engaging in detailed discussions, expressing complex ideas clearly, and using language to explain reasoning and reflect on learning.

Social-Emotional Development

Demonstrating empathy and cooperation in group work, resolving conflicts independently, and showing growing self-awareness and resilience.

Fine-Motor Skills

Writing with increasing fluency and control, and using tools precisely across art, writing, and hands-on projects.

Gross-Motor Skills

Participating skillfully in structured physical activities and demonstrating strong coordination and stamina in group play.

Independence and Self-Help

Managing personal responsibilities, materials, and time with confidence and minimal support.

Early Literacy

Reading simple texts with growing fluency, writing sentences and short stories, and using letter-sound knowledge to decode new words.

Early Math and Problem-Solving

Applying addition and subtraction to real problems, recognizing and extending complex patterns, and using logical reasoning in hands-on investigations.

Creativity, Critical Thinking, and Hands-On Learning

Designing and completing in-depth projects, asking research-style questions, and applying creative and analytical thinking across subjects.

Classroom Readiness

Working independently and in groups, following multi-step academic routines, and demonstrating the focus, organization, and self-direction expected in elementary school.
